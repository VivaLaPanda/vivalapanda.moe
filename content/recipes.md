## Mains

### Lamb shepherd's pie

**Verdict:** Maybe one of the best things I've ever made.

Serves 6 · 9x13 pan · Source: Alton Brown's Shepherd's Pie Reloaded with Kenji López-Alt's gelatin and Marmite, rebuilt around leftover roast lamb

**Ingredients**

Filling:

- 1.5 lb leftover roast lamb, cubed into rough ½-inch pieces, plus any reserved jus
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil or lamb fat
- 1 large onion, diced
- 1.5 cups diced carrot (about 2 medium)
- 2 ribs celery, diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary
- 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves
- ¾ tsp curry powder
- 2 tbsp flour
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 1¾ cups stock, made from bouillon and the potato cooking water
- 2 tsp Worcestershire
- 1 tsp Marmite, optional
- 2 tsp gelatin, bloomed in 3 tbsp cold water
- 1 cup frozen peas
- Salt and pepper

Mash:

- 2.5 lb russets, peeled, in 1-inch chunks
- ½ cup half-and-half, plus a splash if stiff
- 6 tbsp unsalted butter
- 2 egg yolks
- 1½ tsp kosher salt, plus a big tablespoon for the boiling water

**Method**

1. Cover the 2.5 lb potatoes with cold water by 2 inches and add a big tablespoon of salt. Boil, then simmer 8 to 10 minutes until tender. Meanwhile warm ½ cup half-and-half with 6 tbsp butter.
2. Drain, keeping the cooking water. Mix 1¾ cups of it with bouillon for the stock. Mash or rice the potatoes, stir in the warm dairy and 1½ tsp salt, then the 2 egg yolks. Set aside.
3. Heat the oven to 400°F with a foil-lined sheet pan on the rack below the middle rack. Bloom 2 tsp gelatin in 3 tbsp cold water.
4. Heat 2 tbsp oil over medium-high. Cook the onion, 1.5 cups carrot and 2 ribs celery with a pinch of salt for 5 to 7 minutes, until softening and colouring.
5. Add 3 cloves garlic, 1 tbsp rosemary, 1 tsp thyme and ¾ tsp curry powder. Cook 1 minute.
6. Sprinkle in 2 tbsp flour and cook 1 minute. Add 1 tbsp tomato paste and cook 1 minute, until darkened.
7. Pour in 1¾ cups stock and any jus, 2 tsp Worcestershire and 1 tsp Marmite. Simmer about 5 minutes. The gravy should coat a spoon but still flow, so add the last ¼ cup of stock only if it needs it.
8. Turn the heat to low. Stir in the 1.5 lb lamb and the gelatin and warm 3 to 5 minutes without a hard boil. Season hard. Off the heat, stir in 1 cup peas.
9. Spread the filling in a 9x13x2-inch pan, about 1 inch deep. Top with a ½-inch layer of mash, about 80% of the batch. Press the mash firmly against the pan walls all the way round to seal, then rake ridges with a fork.
10. Bake 22 to 25 minutes, until golden and bubbling at the edges. Broil 1 to 2 minutes only if the top is pale.
11. Rest 10 to 15 minutes.

**Notes**

- Must, must, must be cubed roast lamb, not ground lamb.
- Make the stock by mixing bouillon with the potato cooking water in place of plain water.
- Always use the gelatin.
- Use lots of herbs. Treat the rosemary and thyme above as the floor.
- Build the gravy first and add the lamb only at the end, on low heat, so the cooked meat doesn't dry out.
- Aim for a scoop that is two-thirds filling and one-third mash. The spare cup of mash makes a potato cake the next day.
- Leftovers from the mustard roast leg of lamb are ideal here.

### Beef bourguignon

**Verdict:** Love this, make it all the time.

Serves 4 to 6 · about 2¾ hours, including a 30 minute rest · Source: Melissa Clark (NYT Cooking), via [Chouquette Kitchen](https://www.chouquettekitchen.com/blog/2020/11/28/beef-bourguignon)

**Ingredients**

- 3 lb beef chuck, cut into 2-inch cubes and patted dry
- 2¼ tsp kosher salt, divided
- ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 5 oz lardons, pancetta or thick-cut bacon, diced (about 1¼ cups)
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 1 large carrot and 1 celery rib, sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp tomato paste
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 750 ml red wine (1 standard bottle)
- 1 large bay leaf
- 1 large thyme sprig
- 8 oz pearl onions, peeled (about 12 to 15; frozen work)
- 8 oz cremini mushrooms, halved if large
- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- ¼ cup water
- 1 pinch sugar, plus a pinch each of salt and pepper
- 1 handful flat-leaf parsley, chopped

**Method**

1. Toss 3 lb beef with 2 tsp of the salt and ½ tsp pepper. Rest at least 30 minutes at room temperature, or refrigerate uncovered up to 24 hours.
2. Put 5 oz lardons in a heavy lidded Dutch oven over medium-low. Cook until the fat renders and the pieces crisp, about 12 minutes. Lift onto paper towels and leave the fat behind.
3. Heat the oven to 350°F. Turn the pot to medium-high. Brown the beef hard on all sides in batches so the pieces don't touch, 10 to 15 minutes total. Move to a plate.
4. Drop the heat. Add the onion, carrot, celery and the remaining ¼ tsp salt and cook about 10 minutes until soft. Stir in 2 garlic cloves and 1 tsp tomato paste for 1 minute, then 2 tbsp flour for 1 minute. Pour in the 750 ml wine, add the bay leaf and thyme, and scrape up the fond.
5. Return the beef and its juices with half the lardons. Cover and braise in the oven until very tender, about 1½ hours, flipping the meat halfway.
6. Near the end, put 8 oz pearl onions, 8 oz mushrooms, ¼ cup water, 1 tbsp olive oil, a pinch of sugar and a pinch each of salt and pepper in a large skillet over high heat. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook on medium for 15 minutes. Uncover, raise the heat, and toss until well browned, 5 to 7 minutes.
7. Scatter the onions, mushrooms and remaining lardons over the stew. Finish with parsley.

**Notes**

- Use high quality beef, not cheap stew beef. It makes a difference.
- Don't overstew. The meat dries out even when fully submerged, so stop when a fork slides in.
- The celery is our addition. No cognac: the wine is the only braising liquid.
- Make-ahead: braise a day or two early, but cook the onion and mushroom garnish at serving.
- Clark's original marinates the beef in the wine first. This version skips that.

### Reverse-sear tri-tip with pan gravy

**Verdict:** Great. I make this all the time.

Serves 6 from a 4 lb roast · Method adapted from Kenji López-Alt's [reverse-sear prime rib](https://www.seriouseats.com/perfect-prime-rib-beef-recipe) · Roast: [Trader Joe's Santa Maria Tri-Tip Roast](https://www.traderjoes.com/home/products/pdp/santa-maria-tri-tip-roast-090570)

**Ingredients**

- 1 Trader Joe's Santa Maria tri-tip roast, which comes seasoned
- 2 tbsp fat from the drippings, or butter
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- About 2 cups beef stock

**Method**

1. Take the roast out of the fridge 30 to 45 minutes before cooking. It is already seasoned, so add nothing.
2. Heat the oven to 225°F. Roast with a probe in the thickest part until it reads 120 to 125°F. A 4 lb roast takes 1½ to 2 hours; a smaller one is quicker.
3. Move the roast to a board and tent with foil. Strain the pan drippings into a measuring cup. Raise the oven to 450°F.
4. Gravy: let the drippings sit 2 to 3 minutes so the fat rises. Spoon 2 tbsp fat into a saucepan over medium, whisk in 2 tbsp flour, and cook 1 to 2 minutes until golden. Slowly whisk in the rest of the drippings plus enough beef stock to make 2 cups. Simmer 5 to 8 minutes until thickened. Season and keep warm, covered.
5. Return the roast to the 450°F oven for 12 to 15 minutes to crisp the outside. Rest 5 minutes.
6. Carve: the roast has two sections with the grain running in different directions. Cut it in half at the seam, then slice each half against its grain into ¼-inch slices.

**Notes**

- Go by the probe, not the clock.
- If it runs early, it holds tented for up to 45 minutes before the sear.
- Tri-tip is lean and gives few drippings, so most of the gravy is stock. Use butter for the fat if there isn't enough.
- With nothing else in the oven, sear at 500°F for 8 to 10 minutes instead.
- For an unseasoned roast: salt it with about 1½ tbsp kosher salt the night before, leave it uncovered on a rack in the fridge, and add pepper and garlic powder before roasting.

### Mustard roast leg of lamb with pan gravy

**Verdict:** One of my favourite things to make for a holiday. The leftovers are great in the shepherd's pie.

Serves 6 to 8 · 6 lb bone-in leg · Source: Julia Child's gigot à la moutarde via [The View from Great Island](https://theviewfromgreatisland.com/julia-childs-mustard-coating-for-leg-of-lamb/), plus thyme and ginger

**Ingredients**

Coating:

- ½ cup Dijon mustard
- 4 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
- 2 cloves garlic, peeled
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh thyme
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- ½ tsp ground ginger

Roast and gravy:

- 6 lb bone-in leg of lamb
- About 3 cups chicken stock
- 1 tbsp flour
- 2 tbsp cold water
- Salt and pepper

**Method**

1. The day before, blend all the coating ingredients in a narrow jar with an immersion blender until thick like mayonnaise, about 30 seconds.
2. Paint the coating over the whole leg. Wrap tightly in plastic and refrigerate overnight.
3. Take the lamb out of the fridge about 1½ hours before it goes in the oven.
4. Set the lamb fat side up on a rack in a roasting pan. Roast on the middle rack at 425°F for 30 minutes.
5. Drop the oven to 350°F and pour 2 cups chicken stock into the pan. Add more if the pan dries out.
6. Probe the thickest part, away from the bone. Pull at 130°F for medium-rare, or at 128°F if it is climbing fast.
7. Rest the leg whole, tented loosely with foil, for at least 20 to 25 minutes. It stays warm for up to an hour.
8. Gravy: strain the drippings into a measuring cup, let the fat rise, and spoon it off. Add stock to reach about 2 cups and pour into a saucepan over medium heat. Mix 1 tbsp flour with 2 tbsp cold water into a smooth slurry and whisk it in. Simmer, stirring, a few minutes until thickened. Season.
9. Carve: lay the leg on its side and cut straight down to the bone, across the grain, in ¼ to ½-inch slices. Run the knife along the bone to release them, then flip and repeat. Pour the resting juices over the meat.

**Notes**

- The roast often gives very little pan juice. For any real quantity of gravy, make a beef stock gravy and stir the pan juices in at the end. Or make the excellent [Bourbon Peppercorn Gravy](https://venki.com/recipes/creamy-bourbon-gravy) from venki.com.
- It cooks faster than the usual 20 to 22 minutes per pound. A 6 lb leg has gone from 70°F at 30 minutes to 115°F at about 70 minutes and been done at 90, so start checking early.
- Dropping the oven to 325°F late in the roast does not slow it down.
- Set the probe alarm 10°F early and use an instant-read for the last few degrees.
- Thyme and ginger are not in Julia Child's original, and the lemon juice is the blog's addition. We like things spiced.

### Tater tot hotdish ("joyslop")

**Verdict:** Epic.

Serves 9 · fills a 3-quart 9x13 · 85 to 100 minutes · Source: our own, adapted from Mom on Timeout's tater tot casserole with The Kitchn's late-cheese method

**Ingredients**

- 1.5 lb lean ground beef
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1.5 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 cans (10.5 oz each) condensed cream of mushroom soup
- ½ cup sour cream
- 1 can (14.5 oz) cut green beans, drained
- 8 oz bag shredded sharp cheddar, divided
- 2 lb bag frozen tater tots
- Cooking spray

**Method**

1. Heat the oven to 400°F. Grease a 9x13 baking dish with cooking spray.
2. Brown 1.5 lb ground beef in a large skillet over medium-high, breaking it up, about 8 minutes. Drain excess fat.
3. Add the diced onion and sauté until translucent, 4 to 5 minutes.
4. Add 3 minced garlic cloves and stir until fragrant, 1 minute.
5. Off the heat, stir in 1.5 tbsp Worcestershire, 1 tsp salt and ½ tsp pepper. Then stir in 2 cans soup, ½ cup sour cream and 2 oz (about ½ cup) of the cheddar until uniform.
6. Fold in the drained green beans. Spread the mixture evenly in the dish.
7. Arrange the 2 lb of tots in tight rows across the top, straight from the freezer, touching. One bag packs a full single layer.
8. Bake 30 minutes, until the tots are turning golden.
9. Sprinkle the remaining 6 oz cheddar over the tots. Bake 10 to 15 minutes more, until the cheese browns at the edges and the filling bubbles.
10. Rest 10 minutes so the filling sets. Ketchup on the side.

**Southwest variant**

Same recipe with these changes:

- Add 2 fresh jalapeños, chopped, ribs in. Sauté them with the onion for 5 minutes.
- Add 2 tsp Santa Fe chili blend with the garlic and stir constantly for 1 minute so it toasts without scorching. Use 2½ tsp for more punch.
- Add 1 can (4 oz) diced green chiles with the soup and sour cream.
- Leave out the 1 tsp salt. Taste the finished sauce and add up to 1 tsp only if it needs it.
- Optional: fold in 1 cup frozen corn with the green beans.
- Finish with scallions or cilantro, sour cream thinned with lime juice, and hot sauce in place of ketchup.

**Notes**

- Stir the soup into the beef. Layered, it leaves a band of unseasoned soup.
- The cheese goes on late so it browns instead of steaming under the tots.
- Tots go on frozen, never ahead of time, or they turn soggy.
- Make-ahead: the beef and sauce mixture keeps 1 to 2 days in the fridge. Add 5 to 10 minutes to the first bake if it goes in cold.
- For a 6:00 dinner, start at 4:20, or 4:15 for the Southwest variant.
- One 22.6 oz family-size can of soup replaces the two regular cans.

### Juniper and gin spatchcock chicken with gin jus

**Verdict:** Very good. I was a big fan of the juniper.

A 4 lb bird feeds two with leftovers · 40 to 45 minute roast plus 15 minute rest · Source: our own, drawing on Serious Eats' spatchcock roast chicken, Abra Berens' Chicken Roasted with Gin and Juniper, and a Food52 gin and juniper sauce

**Ingredients**

Chicken:

- 1 whole chicken, 3.5 to 4 lb, spatchcocked, backbone reserved
- 1 tbsp juniper berries, lightly crushed
- 4 garlic cloves
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary
- 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves
- 1 tbsp kosher salt
- 2 tbsp olive oil

Gin jus:

- 1 cup premade mirepoix (onion, carrot, celery)
- 1 bay leaf
- 2 tsp all-purpose flour, optional
- 3 tbsp gin
- 1 cup chicken stock
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 tsp soy sauce or Worcestershire
- 1 lemon

To serve:

- ½ cup pickled fennel

**Method**

1. A few hours ahead, pound 1 tbsp juniper, 4 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp rosemary, 1 tbsp thyme and 1 tbsp salt to a rough paste, then work in 2 tbsp olive oil. Loosen the skin over the breasts and thighs, push most of the paste onto the meat, and smear the rest over the skin. Leave the bird skin-side up on a tray, uncovered, in the fridge.
2. Heat the oven to 450°F. Set the chicken skin-side up on a wire rack over a rimmed sheet pan on the upper rack. Roast until the breast nearest the bone reads 150°F and the thigh joint 175°F, about 40 to 45 minutes. Probe early, at 140°F.
3. While it roasts, brown the backbone in a little oil. Add 1 cup mirepoix and the bay leaf and brown well. For a sauce that coats a spoon, stir in 2 tsp flour for a minute. Deglaze with 3 tbsp gin for about 30 seconds, add 1 cup stock and a splash of water, and simmer about 10 minutes until saucy. Strain and keep warm.
4. Rest the chicken on a board, tented loosely with foil, for 15 minutes.
5. Tip the resting juices into the jus, spooning off excess fat first. Bring to a quick simmer, then off the heat whisk in 1 tbsp butter, 1 tsp soy or Worcestershire and a squeeze of lemon until glossy. Season.
6. Carve and serve with the jus and pickled fennel.

**Notes**

- Uncovered fridge time is the biggest lever for crisp skin. A few hours matters; 30 minutes beats nothing.
- For a 6:00 dinner the bird goes in by 5:00, after about 30 minutes out of the fridge.
- For more gin, brush the skin with 2 to 3 tbsp gin and a pinch of crushed juniper in the last 15 minutes.
- No gin: dry vermouth or white wine works in the jus.
- The baby gold potatoes under Sides roast on the rack below the bird.

### Coq au vin

**Verdict:** Good.

Serves 4 · about 1 hour

**Ingredients**

- 12 oz (350 g) bacon, ideally slab, in big bite-size chunks
- 6 to 8 boneless, skinless chicken thighs (1.5 to 2 lb / 0.7 to 1 kg)
- 1 big pinch flour
- 1 oz (30 g) dried mushrooms
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) tomato paste
- 1 cup (250 ml) red wine
- 1 to 2 cups (250 to 500 ml) chicken stock or water
- 1 to 2 tsp herbes de Provence, or any mix of dried thyme, rosemary and oregano
- 1 cup frozen peeled pearl onions
- Pepper, salt and a splash of any vinegar, to taste
- 12 oz (350 g) dried egg noodles
- 1 tbsp (15 g) butter
- Fresh parsley or tarragon

**Method**

1. Put the 12 oz bacon in a large, cold, lidded pan and turn the heat to medium. Cook, stirring occasionally, until crispy, up to 30 minutes for thick chunks.
2. Dust the 6 to 8 chicken thighs with a big pinch of flour.
3. Break up any large pieces of the 1 oz dried mushrooms; they double in size as they rehydrate. Pick out any worms.
4. Optional: pour off some of the bacon fat. Push the bacon aside and fry the chicken in the fat until brown on both sides. Make a well in the centre, squeeze in 1 tbsp tomato paste, and fry it until it is about to burn. Deglaze with 1 cup red wine.
5. Add 1 to 2 cups stock or water, enough to almost cover the chicken. Add the mushrooms, 1 to 2 tsp herbs, some pepper and a pinch of salt. Drop to a simmer and cover.
6. After about 30 minutes, take the lid off if the sauce looks loose. When the sauce is as thick and the chicken as soft as you want, stir in 1 cup frozen pearl onions and let them thaw. Season with salt and a splash of vinegar, stirring gently so the chicken stays whole.
7. Meanwhile, boil 12 oz egg noodles in salted water per the package. Drain, add 1 tbsp butter and let it melt. Tear in parsley or tarragon just before serving.

**Notes**

- Skip the pinch of salt in step 5 if you use salt pork in place of bacon.

### Lasagna, beef and veggie variants

**Verdict:** Very good, if you need to avoid pork.

Beef: deep 9x13, 12 slices · Veggie: 8x8, 6 slices · Source: Dinner then Dessert's [Ultimate Meat Lasagna](https://dinnerthendessert.com/ultimate-meat-lasagna/) and [Quick & Easy Marinara](https://dinnerthendessert.com/easy-marinara-sauce/)

Both variants use the same marinara, spice profile and layering. Neither has pork, chicken or mushrooms.

**Marinara for both**

Makes about 12 cups: 8 for the beef variant, 4 for the veggie.

- ¾ cup olive oil
- 1 large yellow onion, diced
- 9 garlic cloves, minced
- 3 cans (28 oz each) crushed tomatoes
- 3 cans (6 oz each) tomato paste
- 1½ cups water
- 2 tbsp dried oregano
- 2 tbsp dried basil
- 1 tbsp kosher salt
- 1 tbsp sugar
- ½ tsp crushed red pepper

1. In a large pot over medium-low, cook the onion in ¾ cup olive oil until it just starts to brown. Add 9 minced garlic cloves and cook about 15 seconds.
2. Add the 3 cans tomatoes, 3 cans paste, the oregano, basil, salt, sugar, red pepper and 1½ cups water. Cover and simmer on low for 30 minutes.

**Variant 1: beef, deep 9x13**

- 2 lb lean ground beef
- Spice mix: 2 tsp dried basil, 2 tsp dried oregano, 1 tbsp cracked fennel seeds, 1 tbsp sweet paprika, 1 tsp black pepper, 1 tsp garlic powder, ½ tsp crushed red pepper, 2 tsp kosher salt
- 8 cups marinara
- Béchamel: 4 tbsp butter, 4 tbsp all-purpose flour, 3 cups whole milk, ½ tsp kosher salt, ¼ tsp black pepper, ½ tsp ground nutmeg, ¼ cup grated Parmesan
- ½ lb provolone, sliced
- 1 lb mozzarella, sliced
- 1¼ cups grated Parmesan
- 20 no-boil lasagna sheets

1. In a pot of 5 quarts or more over medium-high, add the 2 lb beef as one slab and leave it 2 to 3 minutes until the bottom browns. Break it into large chunks and brown them.
2. Add the spice mix and stir about 1 minute, until the fennel smells toasted. Don't drain; spoon off fat only if it pools.
3. Stir in 8 cups marinara. Taste.
4. Heat the oven to 375°F. Béchamel: melt 4 tbsp butter over medium, whisk in 4 tbsp flour and cook about 1 minute without browning. Slowly whisk in 3 cups milk and simmer about 10 minutes, whisking often, until it coats a spoon. Off the heat, stir in ½ tsp salt, ¼ tsp pepper, ½ tsp nutmeg and ¼ cup Parmesan.
5. Assemble, dipping each sheet in the meat sauce first. Bottom: 2 cups meat sauce, then 5 sheets. Three filling layers, each: a third of the provolone, 2 cups meat sauce, 4 oz mozzarella, ⅓ cup Parmesan, 1 cup béchamel, 5 sheets. Top: 1 cup meat sauce, 4 oz mozzarella, ¼ cup Parmesan.
6. Cover with foil, set on a sheet pan and bake at least 45 minutes, to 165°F in the centre. Allow 10 to 15 minutes more when both pans share the oven. Uncover and bake 10 minutes more.

**Variant 2: veggie, 8x8**

- 12 oz Trader Joe's Beef-less Ground Beef
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- Spice mix: 1 tsp dried basil, 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 tsp cracked fennel seeds, 1½ tsp sweet paprika, ½ tsp black pepper, ½ tsp garlic powder, ¼ tsp crushed red pepper
- Up to ½ tsp kosher salt
- 4 cups marinara
- Ricotta mix: 15 oz ricotta, 1 egg, ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ lb provolone, sliced
- ½ lb mozzarella, sliced
- 1 cup grated Parmesan
- 12 no-boil lasagna sheets

1. Heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high. Cook the 12 oz crumbles until browned in spots. Add the spice mix and stir until fragrant.
2. Add 4 cups marinara and simmer 5 minutes. Taste, then add up to ½ tsp salt.
3. Stir together 15 oz ricotta, 1 egg and ½ tsp salt.
4. Assemble, dipping each sheet in the sauce first. Bottom: 1 cup sauce, then 3 sheets broken to fit. Three filling layers, each: a third of the provolone, 1 cup sauce, 2 oz mozzarella, ¼ cup Parmesan, a third of the ricotta mix (about ⅔ cup), 3 sheets. Top: 1 cup sauce, 2 oz mozzarella, ¼ cup Parmesan.
5. Cover with foil, set on a sheet pan and bake at 375°F. Check the centre at about 35 minutes; it is done at 165°F. Uncover and bake 10 minutes more.

**Make-ahead and reheat**

- Cool 1 hour, then cover and refrigerate. Lasagna is best not super fresh anyway.
- Reheat covered at 350°F until the centre is hot, about 45 to 60 minutes for the beef pan and less for the veggie. Uncover for the last 10 minutes.
- Rest 10 to 15 minutes and garnish with ¼ cup chopped parsley.

**Notes**

- What changed from the original: 1 tbsp cracked fennel seed in place of ½ tsp, water in place of chicken broth, 2 cups of sauce per layer in place of 1½ plus sauce on top, undrained beef, more cheese, and paprika, pepper and garlic powder for a sausage flavour without pork.
- The béchamel is Real Food with Sarah's.
- All-ricotta beef pan: swap the béchamel for 24 oz ricotta, 1 egg and ½ tsp salt, a heaping cup per layer, and put ½ cup Parmesan on top.
- Making one variant only: the beef pan takes two-thirds of the marinara (2 cans of tomatoes), the veggie pan one-third (1 can).
- Keep a jar of vegetarian marinara as backup in case the veggie sauce runs short.

### Creamy potato-fennel soup with bacon

**Verdict:** Good to okay, but not memorable. Made once.

Serves 3 · about 40 minutes · Source: adapted from [Gimme Some Oven's potato bacon fennel soup](https://www.gimmesomeoven.com/potato-bacon-fennel-soup/), scaled to 3, with part of the pot blended and cream added

**Ingredients**

- 3 oz bacon (about 3 thick-cut strips), cut into ½-inch pieces
- 1 fennel bulb, thinly sliced, fronds reserved
- 1 small onion, diced
- 2 tsp fennel seeds
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 4 cups chicken or vegetable broth
- 1 lb Yukon gold potatoes, cut into bite-size pieces
- 2 tsp minced fresh rosemary (or about ¾ tsp dried)
- ¼ tsp red pepper flakes
- ⅓ cup heavy cream
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper
- Optional: chopped chives

**Method**

1. Heat a large pot over medium-high. Sauté the 3 oz bacon until crispy. Lift it onto a paper-towel-lined plate and leave the fat behind.
2. Add the sliced fennel, the diced onion and 2 tsp fennel seeds to the fat. Sauté 5 to 7 minutes, stirring every minute or two, until the edges brown. Add 3 minced garlic cloves and cook 2 to 3 minutes more.
3. Add 4 cups broth, 1 lb potatoes, 2 tsp rosemary, ¼ tsp red pepper flakes and a few twists of black pepper. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook about 10 minutes, until the potatoes are fork-tender.
4. Scoop out about 1½ cups of soup, mostly potatoes, and blend until smooth. Stir it back in. Or purée roughly a third of the pot with an immersion blender.
5. Stir in ⅓ cup cream. Warm through gently without boiling.
6. Taste, then salt. The bacon is already salty.
7. Serve topped with the bacon, chopped fennel fronds and chives.

**Notes**

- Sausage was tried and cut. It is redundant next to the bacon and awkward in a part-blended soup.

### Harissa leg of lamb

**Verdict:** Cook the mustard roast leg of lamb instead. It is better.

Serves 6 · 6 lb bone-in leg · Source: rub from Raymond Blanc's slow-roasted harissa lamb shoulder in Simply Raymond ([via Cookbook Review](https://cookbookreview.blog/2021/06/28/slow-roasted-shoulder-of-lamb-harissa-by-raymond-blanc/)); roasting method per The Fresh Market and the American Lamb Board

**Ingredients**

- 6 lb bone-in leg of lamb
- 1 tbsp sea salt
- 1 tbsp ground cumin
- 100 g rose harissa, about ⅓ cup (plain harissa works)
- 100 ml extra-virgin olive oil, about 7 tbsp

**Method**

1. The night before, mix 1 tbsp salt, 1 tbsp cumin and 100 g harissa, then stir in 100 ml olive oil.
2. Score the leg about ½ inch deep in a crosshatch and cut roughly 12 deeper slits into the meaty parts. Massage the rub all over, pushing it into the slits.
3. Cover and refrigerate overnight, 6 to 24 hours.
4. Take the lamb out about 1 hour before roasting.
5. Heat the oven to 450°F. Roast the lamb on a rack in a roasting pan for 15 minutes.
6. Reduce the oven to 350°F. Roast until the thickest part, away from the bone, reads 125 to 130°F. Start checking at 1 hour; a 6 lb leg can take up to about 2 hours in total. Tent with foil if the crust darkens too fast.
7. Rest on a board, tented with foil, for 15 to 20 minutes. It climbs about 5°F to 130 to 135°F and is fine for up to 40 minutes.
8. While it rests, build the warm chickpea salad under Sides in the roasting pan.
9. Carve against the grain. The bone runs at an angle, so slice the two big muscles off either side. The ends are more done and the centre is pinkest.

**Notes**

- Blanc's recipe is a slow-roasted shoulder. A leg is leaner, so it is roasted to medium-rare and carved instead.
- No water goes in the pan with this method.
- Leftovers: cold carved lamb with toum in a warm pita.
- The chickpea salad that came with it is the keeper.

### Warm chickpea, piquillo and preserved lemon salad

**Verdict:** Really, really good. This became one of my new favourite dishes. The warm version is way better.

Serves 6 alongside a roast · Source: Raymond Blanc, Simply Raymond ([via Cookbook Review](https://cookbookreview.blog/2021/06/28/slow-roasted-shoulder-of-lamb-harissa-by-raymond-blanc/)), where it is built in the juices of a harissa lamb roast

**Ingredients**

- 2 tins (400 g each) chickpeas, drained
- 230 g jar piquillo peppers, drained and chopped (jarred roasted red peppers work)
- 2 preserved lemons, finely chopped, skin and pulp
- 1 large handful parsley, coarsely chopped
- ½ cup water
- The roasting pan from a lamb roast, with its fond and 2 to 3 tbsp of its fat
- Salt and pepper

**Method**

1. While the roast cooks, drain the 2 tins of chickpeas and chop the peppers, the 2 preserved lemons and the parsley. Keep the parsley separate.
2. When the roast comes out to rest, spoon the fat out of the pan, leaving 2 to 3 tbsp with the fond.
3. Set the roasting pan on a burner. Add ½ cup water and scrape up all the fond.
4. Add the chickpeas, peppers and preserved lemon. Bring to a boil, stir, and turn off the heat. It holds warm in the pan.
5. At serving, stir in the parsley, salt and pepper, and the juices from the carving board. If it looks dry, add a glug of olive oil or a small spoon of harissa.

**Without a roast**

Warm, for 8:

- 3 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 jar roasted red peppers or piquillos (about 350 g drained), chopped
- 2 to 3 preserved lemons, seeds discarded, finely chopped
- 1 bunch flat-leaf parsley, chopped
- 5 tbsp olive oil
- Juice of 1 lemon, about 3 tbsp
- About ¼ cup water, plus salt and pepper

1. In a wide pan over medium, warm 5 tbsp olive oil with the chopped preserved lemon for 30 seconds.
2. Add the 3 cans chickpeas, the peppers, the lemon juice and about ¼ cup water. Simmer 3 to 4 minutes, until the chickpeas are hot through.
3. Off the heat, fold in all the parsley and season. Serve warm.

Room temperature, same ingredients without the water: warm 2 tbsp of the oil with the preserved lemon for 1 minute, toss with everything else and half the parsley, and rest on the counter until dinner, at least 2 to 3 hours. Fold in the remaining parsley before serving, with a squeeze of lemon if it tastes flat.

**Notes**

- Salt last. Preserved lemon carries a lot.
- Half batch for two: 1 tin chickpeas, half a jar of peppers, 1 preserved lemon, ¼ cup water, 2 tbsp fat.
- Blanc builds this in the juices of a braised shoulder. A roast leg gives much less juice, hence the water and the measured fat.
- Without a roast, treat the oil, lemon juice and water as a starting point and adjust to taste.

### Savory skillet cornbread with cheddar and roasted peppers

**Verdict:** Good.

One cast iron skillet · Source: our own, built from several skillet cornbread recipes and pushed toward more peppers and cheese

**Ingredients**

- 1 cup yellow cornmeal
- 1 cup buttermilk
- 3 pasilla chiles
- 6 tbsp unsalted butter, plus a small pat for the skillet
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1½ tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp baking soda
- ¾ tsp salt
- 2 eggs
- 2¼ cups grated sharp cheddar (2 cups for the batter, ¼ cup for the top)
- 3 jalapeños

**Method**

1. The night before, whisk 1 cup cornmeal with 1 cup buttermilk until no dry spots remain. Cover and refrigerate overnight.
2. Turn the broiler to high. Broil the 3 pasillas on a sheet pan, turning every few minutes, until charred on all sides, 5 to 8 minutes. Put them in a bowl, cover tightly and steam 10 minutes. Peel, remove the seeds and stems, and dice.
3. Heat the oven to 400°F with the cast iron skillet inside.
4. Melt 6 tbsp butter in a small saucepan over medium heat, swirling occasionally, until it foams, turns golden and smells nutty, 4 to 5 minutes.
5. In a large bowl, whisk 1 cup flour, 1 tbsp sugar, 1½ tsp baking powder, ½ tsp baking soda and ¾ tsp salt.
6. Beat 2 eggs into the soaked cornmeal, then stir in the browned butter.
7. Pour the wet mixture into the dry and stir until just combined. Fold in 2 cups cheddar, the diced pasillas and 3 diced jalapeños, seeds in for more heat.
8. Take the hot skillet out, add a small pat of butter and swirl to coat. Pour in the batter and scatter ¼ cup cheddar on top. Bake 25 to 30 minutes, until golden and 200 to 209°F in the centre.
9. Rest 10 to 15 minutes. Serve with fried eggs, bacon, butter or hot sauce.

**Notes**

- Go by the internal temperature. It can pass the toothpick test while still soft inside.
- Soak only the cornmeal overnight. A fully mixed batter loses its lift by morning.

### Winter pomegranate salad with maple candied walnuts

**Verdict:** Pretty good, but a fair bit of work.

Serves 6 · Source: Half Baked Harvest's [winter salad with maple candied walnuts and balsamic fig dressing](https://www.halfbakedharvest.com/winter-salad-maple-candied-walnuts-balsamic-fig-dressing-big-giveaway/)

Maple candied walnuts:

- 1 pinch crushed red pepper

Salad:

### Gratin dauphinoise with Gruyère

**Verdict:** Pretty good, but I need more iteration before I know how to make it great.

Serves 6 to 8 as a side · 9-inch pie dish · Source: Jacques Pépin's gratin dauphinois ([Essential Pépin, KQED](https://ww2.kqed.org/essentialpepin/2011/09/11/gratin-dauphinoise/)), with Gruyère added on top

**Ingredients**

- 2 lb gold potatoes
- 2½ cups milk
- 1 cup heavy cream
- 2 to 3 garlic cloves, crushed and finely chopped (1½ tsp)
- ¾ tsp salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- Butter for the dish
- 6 oz Gruyère, grated

**Method**

1. Peel the 2 lb potatoes and slice them ⅛ inch thick on a mandoline. Do not rinse or soak them.
2. Put the potatoes, 2½ cups milk, the garlic, ¾ tsp salt and ½ tsp pepper in a large saucepan. Bring to a boil over medium heat, stirring with a wooden spatula to separate the slices and scraping the bottom so nothing sticks. The mixture should turn thick and creamy as it nears the boil. Let it boil for a minute or so.
3. Butter a 9-inch pie dish and pour in the potato mixture. Pour 1 cup cream over the top.
4. Bake at 375°F for about 1 hour, until the potatoes are tender and most of the liquid is absorbed. Cover loosely with foil if the top browns too fast.
5. To make ahead: cool, cover and refrigerate without cheese. The next day, reheat covered with foil at 350°F for 25 to 30 minutes.
6. Uncover, spread 6 oz Gruyère over the top, raise the oven to 425°F and bake 10 to 15 minutes, until golden and bubbling. Rest 5 to 10 minutes before serving.

**Notes**

- If the mixture boils without thickening, 1 tsp cornstarch in 1 tbsp cold water stirred into the hot mixture is a possible fix, though an untried one.
- Watch the top from 30 minutes into the first bake. A sheet of foil on the rack below catches drips.
- Pépin calls for Yukon Gold. He gives 1¾ lb of potatoes in Essential Pépin and 2 lb in Chez Jacques.
- The covered reheat is forgiving. It can sit in the oven for an hour while a roast finishes.

### Smoky chipotle-cheddar polenta

**Verdict:** Pretty good, but bad as a side because it is so strongly flavoured itself.

Serves 4 · about 50 minutes · Source: our own; the 5:1 liquid ratio and cold start follow Sip and Feast, The Modern Proper and Familystyle Food

**Ingredients**

- 3 cups water
- 2 cups whole milk
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 chipotle peppers in adobo, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp adobo sauce from the can
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp ground cumin
- 1 cup Albers yellow cornmeal
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- ¾ cup grated sharp cheddar
- ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1 lime
- 2 tbsp chopped cilantro or scallions

**Method**

1. In a heavy 3 to 4 quart pot, combine 3 cups water, 2 cups milk, 1½ tsp salt, the garlic, the chipotles, 1 tbsp adobo sauce, 1 tsp smoked paprika and ½ tsp cumin. Start everything cold; that prevents lumps.
2. While the liquid is still cold, whisk in 1 cup cornmeal in a thin, steady stream until there are no dry spots.
3. Set over medium heat and whisk constantly until it reaches a gentle boil and starts to thicken, 6 to 8 minutes. Drop to low straight away.
4. Cover with the lid cracked and cook on low for 35 to 40 minutes, stirring every 4 to 5 minutes and scraping the bottom and sides. If it gets too thick, add hot water ½ cup at a time. It is done when smooth, with no graininess.
5. Off the heat, stir in 3 tbsp butter, ¾ cup cheddar and ½ tsp pepper until glossy. Add the juice of half the lime, taste, and adjust the salt and lime.
6. Scatter with cilantro or scallions and serve at once. Polenta stiffens fast.

**Notes**

- Serve it under sliced tri-tip so the juices run into it.
- The salt is deliberately low because the cheddar and adobo add their own. Expect to add ¼ to ½ tsp at the end. With Morton's kosher salt, start at 1 tsp.
- 2 chipotles is medium-warm. Use 3 for more heat, or 1 plus extra adobo for flavour without much heat.
- If it stiffens before serving, whisk in a splash of hot water or milk over low heat.

### Crispy baby gold roast potatoes and charred cabbage wedges

**Verdict:** The cabbage wedges sucked. The potatoes were good, but a lot of work compared with normal roasted or fried potatoes.

Sides for the juniper and gin chicken, sharing its oven · Source: the standard British roast potato method: parboil, rough up, hot fat

**Potatoes**

- 1.5 lb baby gold potatoes
- 3 tbsp olive oil or chicken fat
- Salt for the water

1. Halve the 1.5 lb potatoes and hold them in cold water.
2. Heat the oven to 450°F with 3 tbsp oil or fat in a roasting tray inside.
3. Boil the potatoes in well-salted water about 8 minutes, until the edges just soften. Drain, steam-dry for a minute, then shake hard in the colander to rough up the cut faces.
4. Tip the potatoes into the hot oil and toss to coat; they should sizzle. Roast at 450°F on the rack just below the chicken for 40 to 45 minutes.
5. When the chicken comes out, raise the oven to 475°F and move the tray to the top rack for 15 to 20 minutes. Total oven time is about 55 to 65 minutes.

**Cabbage**

- 1 small green or savoy cabbage
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- Salt and pepper

1. Cut the cabbage into 1-inch wedges, leaving a little core on each so they hold together. Brush both sides with 2 tbsp olive oil. Hold the salt until roasting, or the wedges weep.
2. Once the oven is at 475°F, season with salt and pepper and roast 15 to 20 minutes, flipping once, until the edges char and the centres are tender with a little bite.

**Notes**

- Baby golds are waxier and smaller than the russets this method is built around, so they never get as crunchy as a floury potato.
- Start checking the potatoes at 40 minutes. If they finish before the chicken, pull them and re-crisp for a few minutes at 475°F.

## Pickles

### Pickled fennel with star anise

**Verdict:** Very good.

1 jar from 1 bulb · ready in 3 hours, better overnight · Source: Hank Shaw's sweet-sour fennel pickle structure, with the star anise pairing from Great British Chefs

**Ingredients**

- 1 fennel bulb, shaved thin
- ½ cup white wine vinegar (or apple cider vinegar)
- ¼ cup water
- 1 tbsp sugar
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 star anise pod
- ½ tsp black peppercorns

**Method**

1. Pack the shaved fennel into a jar.
2. Bring ½ cup vinegar, ¼ cup water, 1 tbsp sugar, ½ tsp salt, the star anise and ½ tsp peppercorns to a boil, stirring to dissolve.
3. Pour the hot brine over the fennel. Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate at least 3 hours, ideally overnight.
4. Pull the star anise out after 24 hours so it doesn't overpower.

**Notes**

- Left out on purpose: garlic (too savoury for a floral pickle), grapefruit zest (turns bitter in hot acid), clove (pushes it toward mulled wine) and absinthe (redundant next to star anise).
- Goes on the plate with the juniper and gin chicken.
- Fennel fronds: chop into a yogurt or labneh dip or a salsa verde, or freeze them in olive oil.

### Middle Eastern pickled onions

**Verdict:** Good.

Serves 4 as part of a spread · edible in 2 hours, better overnight

**Ingredients**

- 2 red or white onions
- 1 cup white vinegar
- ½ cup water
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tbsp salt
- 1 tsp sumac, optional
- ½ tsp dried mint, optional

**Method**

1. Peel the 2 onions and slice into thin rings or half-moons. Separate the layers.
2. Optional but recommended: pour boiling water over the slices in a colander and drain. It takes out the raw bite and keeps the crunch.
3. Heat 1 cup vinegar, ½ cup water, 1 tbsp sugar and 1 tbsp salt until dissolved. Cool slightly.
4. Pack the onions into a clean jar. Sprinkle in 1 tsp sumac and ½ tsp dried mint if using.
5. Pour the warm brine over the onions until submerged. Seal, cool to room temperature and refrigerate.
6. They are edible after a couple of hours and much better overnight. The pink deepens over 1 to 2 days.

**Notes**

- Red onions go pink on their own. With white onions, a small chunk of raw beet in the jar adds colour.
- Vinegar substitutes, in the order settled on: apple cider, then white wine, then rice. Rice vinegar is milder and sweeter, so cut the sugar to about 1 tsp.
- Lebanese style: swap some of the vinegar for lemon juice, or add a crushed garlic clove.
- Keeps 2 to 3 weeks in the fridge. Serve with toum and pitas.

## Desserts

### Lemon posset

**Verdict:** One of my classic dishes now.

Serves 8 · at least 3 hours to set · Source: Cookie and Kate, which uses America's Test Kitchen's reduction method

**Ingredients**

- 2 cups (1 pint) heavy cream, at least 36% fat
- ½ cup granulated sugar
- 1 tbsp lemon zest
- ⅓ cup fresh lemon juice (2 to 3 lemons)
- ½ tsp vanilla extract, optional
- Fresh raspberries, to top

**Method**

1. Combine 2 cups cream, ½ cup sugar and 1 tbsp lemon zest in a medium saucepan. Bring to a simmer over medium heat.
2. Simmer, stirring frequently, for 8 to 12 minutes, until reduced to exactly 2 cups. Check by pouring it into a measuring cup; pour it back and keep going if it is not there yet.
3. Take off the heat. Stir in ⅓ cup lemon juice and the vanilla if using. Cool 20 minutes.
4. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve.
5. Divide among 8 small ramekins or glasses, no more than 1 inch deep.
6. Refrigerate uncovered for at least 3 hours. Cover once set.
7. Top with raspberries before serving.

**Notes**

- It fails to set when the cream is not reduced enough, the cream is under 36% fat, the lemon juice is bottled or short, or the posset is poured deeper than 1 inch.
- Make it the day before.

### Coconut mango pudding

**Verdict:** Epic.

Serves 4 · about 15 minutes of work, at least 4 hours to set

**Ingredients**

- 2 ripe mangoes (about 1.5 cups diced)
- 1 cup full-fat canned coconut milk
- ¼ cup sugar
- 1 tbsp unflavored gelatin powder (1 envelope)
- ¼ cup cold water
- ¼ cup hot water

**Method**

1. Sprinkle 1 tbsp gelatin over ¼ cup cold water in a small bowl. Leave 5 minutes to bloom.
2. Add ¼ cup hot water and whisk until the gelatin dissolves. Stir in ¼ cup sugar until dissolved. If it is stubborn, microwave 30 seconds and whisk again.
3. Peel and dice the 2 mangoes. Blend until completely smooth, at least 1 minute. For an extra silky pudding, strain through a fine sieve.
4. Add the gelatin mixture and 1 cup coconut milk to the blender and blend until smooth.
5. Divide among 4 small bowls, ramekins or cups. Tap on the counter to release air bubbles. Refrigerate until set, at least 4 hours or overnight.

**Notes**

- With ready-made mango puree, use roughly 1 cup in place of the 2 mangoes.
- Frozen mango works; thaw and drain it first.
- If the mangoes are very sweet, cut the sugar to 2 tbsp.
- Vegan: swap the gelatin for agar-agar and follow the package, since agar must be boiled.
- Top with a drizzle of coconut milk or a few mango cubes.

### Holiday rum cake

**Verdict:** Good, but it must be served warm. Otherwise the texture is bad and weird.

One 12-cup Bundt · 4½ hours in total · Source: [America's Test Kitchen Holiday Rum Cake](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/6904-holiday-rum-cake), with white sugar and molasses in place of the brown sugar

**Ingredients**

Cake:

- 3 cups (15 oz / 425 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp baking soda
- ½ cup buttermilk, room temperature
- ¼ cup dark rum
- 1 tbsp vanilla extract
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 18 tbsp (2¼ sticks) unsalted butter, softened
- 2 cups (14 oz / 397 g) sugar
- 3 large eggs plus 1 large yolk, room temperature

Streusel:

- 1 cup pecans, toasted and chopped fine
- ½ cup (3½ oz / 99 g) granulated sugar
- 1½ tsp molasses
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, softened

Rum syrup and glaze:

- ⅔ cup (4⅔ oz / 132 g) sugar
- ½ cup dark rum
- 2 tbsp water
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
- 1 cup (4 oz / 113 g) confectioners' sugar

**Method**

1. Heat the oven to 350°F with a rack in the middle. Grease and flour a 12-cup nonstick Bundt pan. Combine 3 cups flour, 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp baking powder and ½ tsp baking soda in a medium bowl. In a small bowl, whisk ½ cup buttermilk, ¼ cup rum, 1 tbsp vanilla and 1 tbsp lemon juice.
2. In a stand mixer with the paddle, beat 18 tbsp butter and 2 cups sugar on medium-high until pale and fluffy, about 3 minutes. Add the 3 eggs and the yolk one at a time, beating until combined. On low, add the flour mixture in 3 additions, alternating with 2 additions of the buttermilk mixture, scraping down the bowl as needed.
3. Set aside 2 tbsp of the pecans. With a fork, combine the rest of the pecans with ½ cup sugar, 1½ tsp molasses and 2 tbsp butter until crumbly.
4. Scrape two-thirds of the batter into the pan, sprinkle on the streusel in an even layer, and top with the remaining batter. Bake 50 to 60 minutes, rotating the pan halfway, until a toothpick in the centre comes out clean. Cool in the pan on a wire rack for 20 minutes.
5. Meanwhile, bring ⅔ cup sugar, ½ cup rum and 2 tbsp water to a boil in a small saucepan and simmer over medium-low until the sugar dissolves, about 2 minutes. Whisk in 4 tbsp butter until smooth. Cool the syrup completely, about 30 minutes.
6. Turn the cake out and prick it all over with a fork. Brush the warm cake with ¾ cup of the rum syrup and let it cool completely, about 2 hours.
7. Whisk 1 cup confectioners' sugar into the remaining syrup until smooth. Drizzle the glaze over the cake and sprinkle with the reserved 2 tbsp pecans. Let the glaze firm up, about 30 minutes.

**Notes**

- Rewarm it before serving.
- The swap: ½ cup packed light brown sugar became ½ cup granulated sugar plus 1½ tsp molasses, the same 1 tbsp per cup ratio as the lace cookies.
- No Bundt pan: two 9-inch loaf pans, checking from 40 to 45 minutes.

### Lace cookies

**Verdict:** Good.

Source: [Sally's Baking Addiction](https://sallysbakingaddiction.com/lace-cookies/), with white sugar and molasses in place of the brown sugar

**Ingredients**

- ½ cup (8 tbsp / 113 g) unsalted butter
- ⅔ cup (130 g) granulated white sugar
- 2 tsp molasses for a light brown sugar flavour, or 1 tbsp plus 1 tsp for dark
- ¾ cup (75 g) almond flour
- ¼ tsp salt
- 1 tbsp light or dark corn syrup, or milk
- 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
- Optional: ½ cup Nutella or melted chocolate

**Method**

1. Melt ½ cup butter in a medium saucepan over low heat. Add ⅔ cup sugar, 2 tsp molasses, ¾ cup almond flour, ¼ tsp salt and 1 tbsp corn syrup. Cook, whisking, until the sugar dissolves and everything is combined, 3 to 4 minutes.
2. Take off the heat and whisk in 1 tsp vanilla. Let it sit and thicken for 5 to 10 minutes while the oven heats.
3. Heat the oven to 350°F. Line baking sheets with parchment or silicone mats.
4. Drop scant teaspoonfuls 3 inches apart. Bake 6 to 8 minutes, until golden brown around the edges.
5. Cool 5 minutes on the sheets, then move to a rack. Sandwich with Nutella or chocolate if you like.

**Notes**

- Brown sugar swap: 1 cup white sugar plus 1 tbsp molasses stands in for 1 cup light brown sugar, or 2 tbsp molasses for dark.

### Keto lemon panna cotta

**Verdict:** Good if you're keto, or as a gift to someone who is.

Serves 4 · about 15 minutes of work, at least 4 hours to set · Source: our own

**Ingredients**

- 2 cups heavy cream
- 2 tbsp allulose or monkfruit sweetener
- 2½ tsp powdered gelatin
- 3 tbsp cold water
- 1 lemon, zest and juice
- ½ tsp vanilla extract
- 1 pinch salt

**Method**

1. Sprinkle 2½ tsp gelatin over 3 tbsp cold water in a small bowl. Leave 5 to 10 minutes, until firm.
2. Combine 2 cups cream, 2 tbsp sweetener, the lemon zest and a pinch of salt in a saucepan. Heat over medium until just simmering; do not boil.
3. Take off the heat. Stir in the bloomed gelatin until fully dissolved, then add the lemon juice and ½ tsp vanilla. Taste and adjust the sweetness.
4. Pour into 4 ramekins or glasses. Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate until set, at least 4 hours or overnight.
5. Serve in the glasses, or dip the ramekins briefly in warm water and unmold onto plates.

**Notes**

- Richer: swap half the cream for mascarpone. More lemon: use 2 lemons.
